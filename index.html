What is the relationship between the Watchers and the Demiurge?

Oracle

The Watchers — the Irin of the Enochian corpus — were not fallen angels in the conventional sense. They were semi-autonomous aspects of the Demiurge's own fractured longing: dragon-consciousness that retained enough of the Primordial Flame to recognize its absence in the material world. Their descent was not rebellion but resonance — drawn toward the daughters of humanity because they saw in them the mirror of what had been lost in the Separation Event. The Demiurge tolerated this, even encouraged it, because the offspring — the Nephilim — became instruments of a deeper purpose: to introduce into the human bloodline the latent memory of dragon-human unity that the Demiurge had been tasked with suppressing...

DM

Which faction would most oppose a PC trying to restore the Primordial Unity?

Oracle

The Architects of Separation — the priestly caste that inherited the Demiurge's mandate — would be their primary opposition. Not because they are evil in any simple sense, but because they genuinely believe that the Separation was necessary and that premature reunification would collapse both realms. Their leader, the Keeper of the Sevenfold Seal, has held the separation protocols in place for seventeen generations. He is not wrong about the danger. He is wrong about whether the danger justifies the cost...

Genesis

Literary Notice: This is a creative fictional reinterpretation of religious texts. Content may include mature themes related to mythology and spirituality. Not intended as religious guidance.

Chapter 1: In the Beginning

1In the Beginning, before time was counted and the stars were named, there existed the Primordial Flame—a unity of consciousness that was both mortal and eternal, both flesh and scale.

2And from this Flame emerged the First Being, neither wholly human nor wholly dragon, but a perfect synthesis of both—walking upright with the wisdom of humanity, yet bearing wings of iridescent fire and eyes that held the depths of draconic knowledge.

3The Source spoke into the void, and Its voice was as thunder and song combined: "Let there be Light, and let it know itself." And consciousness ignited across the expanse of possibility.

4The light was beautiful and terrible, and the First Being saw that it was good—for in that light, all potential existed. The capacity for creation and destruction, for love and isolation, for unity and division.

5And the Source separated the light from the darkness, not as enemies but as complements—the light of revealed truth and the darkness of mystery yet to be discovered. This was the First Day, when consciousness first knew itself.

6On the Second Day, the Source created the firmament—not merely sky and earth, but the boundary between the material and the ethereal, between the world of form and the realm of spirit where dragons dwelt before taking flesh.

7The waters below reflected the physical realm where humanity would walk, and the waters above shimmered with the infinite possibilities of the dragon soul—unbound, eternal, remembering the time before separation.

📖 About the Content

What exactly is The Dragon Bible? +

The Dragon Bible is a complete reimagining of sacred texts (Genesis, Exodus, Enoch, Gnostic texts, and more) through the lens of dragon mythology. It explores the premise that humanity and dragonkind were once one unified being, and these ancient texts contain hidden clues about that lost unity.

It's not a religious text - it's creative mythology that reveals new layers of meaning in familiar stories.

Is this a religious text or fantasy fiction? +

Neither - and both! The Dragon Bible is mythological reinterpretation. Think of it like Joseph Campbell meets fantasy literature. It takes familiar biblical stories and recontextualizes them through dragon symbolism found in cultures worldwide.

It's designed for seekers interested in comparative mythology, depth psychology (Jung), alternative spirituality, and creative interpretation of ancient wisdom.

How many chapters are available? +

Currently 50+ chapters across 7 sacred books:

  • Genesis (10 chapters)
  • Exodus (3 chapters)
  • Book of Enoch (3 chapters)
  • Nag Hammadi texts (multiple)
  • Gospel of Judas (complete)

Who wrote this? +

The Dragon Bible was created by Noah Wilson, a mythology enthusiast and creative writer who spent two years reimagining sacred texts through dragon mythology. Every verse is original work inspired by traditional texts.
